The move 21...Kg7

Legality/safety: it's perfectly safe. The g2-bishop is blocked by its own e4-pawn, the d2-queen has no route to g7, and the f1-rook is blocked by f2. There's no tactical refutation (e.g. Qh6+ just loses the queen to Kxh6, and Qc3+ is impossible because d4 blocks the diagonal).

Purpose: it's a "tidy-up" move – getting off the back rank, avoiding future back-rank issues, and perhaps preparing ...h5 or freeing the f8-rook. There's nothing wrong with the idea in the abstract.

Why it's not the best choice here

Black is a pawn down, and in such positions you need to create problems for the opponent rather than spend a tempo on prophylaxis. Kg7 does nothing about White's main plan of advancing the centre (e5 or d5), which will open the g2-bishop and give White a free hand. After 21...Kg7 22.e5! (or 22.d5) White is comfortably better.

Better, more active options:

  • 21...Rc4! – hits d4 directly (only defended by the queen), keeps e4 under pressure from the a8-queen, and prepares ...Rfc8 or ...Nc6. The rook cannot be chased easily (no White c-pawn, bishop blocked). This is probably the most testing move.
  • 21...Nc6 – attacks d4 at once. If 22.d5, then 22...Ne5 or 22...Nd4 gives Black a monster knight and the c-file. If White defends with 22.Rad1 or 22.Rfd1, Black continues ...Rc4 or ...e5.
  • 21...e5 – strikes at the centre immediately; 22.d5 gives Black a good knight vs the blocked bishop, and 22.dxe5 dxe5 leaves White's e4 pawn permanently attacked by the queen.

Verdict

21...Kg7 is a legal and safe move, but it is passive and wastes a tempo at a moment when Black must generate activity to fight for compensation. Not a blunder – more like an inaccuracy (roughly losing half a pawn of evaluation). I'd prefer ...Rc4 or ...Nc6, which put concrete pressure on White's centre.

* 2... d5! also perfectly blocks the diagonal with a pawn, blunting the g2 bishop entirely.

  • The d5 Break:
